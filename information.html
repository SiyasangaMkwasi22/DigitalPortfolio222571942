Professional Networking

Situation:

As an IT student intern at an IT company, I was part of a development team consisting of other junior developers and a team leader who supervised our tasks. Our team worked collaboratively on various software development projects, where communication, collaboration, and continuous learning were crucial for success. During the internship, I realized that beyond completing technical tasks, it was equally important to build professional relationships within the team and the broader organization — to learn from others, share ideas, and establish networks that could support my long-term career growth.

Task:
My goal was to develop professional networking skills within a real-world environment by engaging with my peers, senior developers, and team leaders. Specifically, I aimed to: Strengthen professional relationships within my team. Connect with experienced professionals to gain mentorship and career insights. Build a network that could be beneficial for future job opportunities and collaboration. Demonstrate professionalism and initiative in communication, both in-person and on professional platforms like LinkedIn.

Action
To achieve this, I implemented several professional networking strategies:
Active Collaboration: I engaged regularly with my teammates during stand-up meetings and project discussions. I made an effort to ask thoughtful questions, share feedback, and offer support when others encountered challenges.
Mentorship Building: I approached my team leader to seek advice on improving my technical and teamwork skills. I requested regular feedback sessions and showed appreciation for their guidance.
Cross-Team Networking: I initiated brief conversations with developers from other teams to understand their roles, technologies, and experiences. This helped me expand my understanding of the company’s structure and culture.
Professional Online Presence: I created and maintained a LinkedIn profile, where I connected with team members and shared insights about what I was learning during my internship.
Continuous Communication: I followed up on conversations, thanked colleagues for their help, and maintained positive interactions throughout my internship.


Result
Through these networking efforts: I built strong working relationships with my team members and gained valuable mentorship from my team leader. I received positive feedback on my collaboration and communication skills. I was invited to participate in future project discussions, showing that my presence and contributions were valued. My LinkedIn network expanded to include IT professionals from the company and beyond, giving me access to future career opportunities. Most importantly, I developed confidence in professional interactions — understanding how to engage respectfully, follow up, and contribute meaningfully to a professional environment.

Business Communication

Situation:

During my internship at an IT company, I worked as part of a development team consisting of several junior developers and a team leader overseeing our projects. Our team collaborated on various software development tasks, including debugging, feature implementation, and testing. I quickly realized that technical ability alone was not enough to ensure productivity or success — clear and professional business communication was vital for coordinating work , avoiding misunderstandings, and maintaining a positive team dynamic.

Task:
My objective was to develop and apply effective business communication skills in both written and verbal contexts. This included:
Communicating progress and technical issues clearly during team meetings.
Writing concise and professional messages on internal communication platforms (e.g., Slack, Microsoft Teams, or email).
Understanding and using the appropriate tone, structure, and formality depending on the audience (team leader, senior developers, or clients).
Listening actively and providing constructive feedback during collaborative discussions.


Action:
To achieve this, I implemented the following communication strategies:
Structured Updates: I prepared and delivered clear status updates during daily stand-up meetings, focusing on what I completed, what I was working on, and what challenges I faced.
Professional Messaging: When communicating via email or chat, I ensured that my messages were concise, respectful, and well-formatted — including greetings, context, and clear requests or responses.
Active Listening: I practiced active listening during team discussions, ensuring I understood instructions before acting. I would often rephrase key points to confirm understanding.
Technical Documentation: I contributed to the project’s documentation by writing explanatory notes and commit messages that other developers could easily follow.
Feedback and Collaboration: I gave and received feedback constructively, ensuring my tone was professional and solution-focused.
Cross-Department Interaction: Occasionally, I interacted with project managers and designers, which taught me to adjust my communication style to suit non-technical audiences.

Result
As a result of improving my business communication skills:
My communication became clearer and more confident, resulting in fewer misunderstandings during development tasks.
My team leader acknowledged my progress in communicating updates and documenting code changes effectively.
Collaboration with other developers became smoother, and I was often approached for clarification or input on shared tasks.
I developed greater professionalism in digital communication, understanding workplace etiquette and tone.
These experiences strengthened my ability to translate technical concepts into simple language, which is essential for dealing with clients or multidisciplinary teams.
